### Scene 7: Taxonomic Hierarchy - Levels of Classification

*   **Visual Description:** A clear, vertical pyramid or nested boxes showing the hierarchy: Kingdom at the top, down to Species at the bottom. Each level (taxon) is highlighted as the voiceover mentions it.
    *   **K**ingdom
    *   **P**hylum (or Division for plants)
    *   **C**lass
    *   **O**rder

*   **Voiceover Text:** "Imagine a filing system for all living things! That's the Taxonomic Hierarchy. It's a series of graded ranks, from the broadest category, Kingdom, all the way down to the most specific, Species. Each level is called a 'taxon'. Remember this order: Kingdom, Phylum, Class, Order, Family, Genus, Species. A handy mnemonic is 'Keep Pond Clean Or Fish Get Sick'!"

### Scene 9: Binomial Nomenclature - The Universal Language

*   **Visual Description:** A split screen. Left: *Homo sapiens* (text with example). Right: *Mangifera indica* (text with example).
    *   Rules text appears:
        1.  Genus first, then species.
        2.  Genus capitalized, species lowercase.
        3.  Latin origin, italicized (or underlined if handwritten).
        4.  Author's name abbreviated at the end (e.g., *Homo sapiens* Linn.).
*   **Voiceover Text:** "To avoid confusion, Carl Linnaeus also gave us Binomial Nomenclature – a universal two-name system! The first name is the Genus, always capitalized. The second is the species, always lowercase. Both are Latin, so they're italicized when typed, or underlined if handwritten. For example, humans are *Homo sapiens*, and the mango tree is *Mangifera indica*."
